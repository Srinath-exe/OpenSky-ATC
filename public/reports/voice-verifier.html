OpenATC · voice model · verifier

Teaching the voice model to know when it is wrong

The in-browser S1 model turns what you said into a command. Sometimes it is confidently wrong. A cheap verifier now checks every proposal against what was heard, and decides: send it, let you check it, or ask you to say again.

58→4

wrong commands sent on their own, out of 423 model proposals on real recogniser transcripts the rules could not parse (today's confidence gate → the verifier). The verifier still sends 89% of the correct ones (today: 92%); the rest wait in the command bar for one keypress.

The problem

High confidence is not the same as right

DEMO
“973 fly heading 102”
BAW963 Fly heading 102model confidence 97% · would go to the wrong aircraft (UPS973 was meant)
WHISPER
“Japan Air 88819 Right Taxi”
JAL888 Taxi RWY 09Rmodel confidence 100% · the wrong runway (19 right was said)

The model's confidence is the probability of the callsign tokens it chose, so it measures how sure the decoder was, not whether the line agrees with the words. A second, independent check is needed: the cheapest one is to say the command back and compare.

How it works

Generator, then verifier

STT

Transcript

“speedbird nine six three turn heading one zero two please”

L0 · rules

Parser

Parses 50–75% of transcripts on its own (those are sent, no model).

L1 · S1 2M

Generator

Writes the command line, callsign constrained to aircraft in the state. Also returns per-token signals.

verify.ts

Verifier

Round trip + signals → small MLP (16 units) → P(correct), then veto rules.

round trip

Say it back

The engine's own phraseology renders the proposal (ICAO + FAA, FL + feet); both sides are normalised (“one zero two” → 102) and aligned: flight number, airline name, every number, left/right, and whether another aircraft fits better.

signals

Decoder evidence

Callsign margin in the constrained trie, lowest token probability, digit-token margin, P(SAY AGAIN). Free: same decode, no extra steps.

gate

Three outcomes

send P ≥ 0.96 and no veto · check (left in the bar) · say again P < 0.15. Vetoes (a heard runway side missing, left↔right, another aircraft fits the words better, a number nobody said) and a callsign the rules had to correct are always check.

Cost after release: 0.15 ms median for the verifier (round trip + model; whole decision 0.23 ms, node, 400 real-recogniser proposals). The weights (30 features, 513 numbers, ~8 KB) are compiled into the page: no download. The S1 decode only reads probabilities it already computes.

Positive = evidence the proposal is right. The shipped combiner is the MLP (it ranked better on the dev fold: AUROC 0.949 vs 0.932); the linear weights are shown because they can be read. Fitted on 23275 training-session proposals (622 wrong; 0 perturbed hard negatives were tried and not used: they did not help on dev).

Parity on 200 transcripts: the engine's default decoder (callsign by likelihood) produced the same line as PyTorch in 200 and every signal matched PyTorch within tolerance in 200; its greedy-trie mode produced the same tokens and confidence as the committed engine in 200. complete() 133.8 ms vs 127.3 ms in trie mode (node, median).

Reproduce

ml/verify/refit.sh [public/models/s1/<name>] rebuilds everything on this page for a new S1 checkpoint (CPU, about an hour): generator pass, labels with the game's own parser, fit, thresholds into src/lib/voice/verify-model.ts, parity check, this report. Details: ml/verify/RESULTS.md.
