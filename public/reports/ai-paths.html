Training loss keeps falling while validation flattens at ~65%: the model memorises the training sessions. The fix is more varied data and a bigger model, i.e. the GPU run.

A big gap between the two bars makes a good “say again” gate. Temperature scaling (one number, fitted on other commands) fixed our model: wrong answers went from 0.90 to 0.41 confidence.

Kev-0.8B, untrained on ATC, matches our trained model on aircraft and is the best at knowing when it is wrong, but it is 40× slower on CPU. Our tiny model is fast and accurate. Next: fine-tune Kev on our data (GPU), and keep the tiny model for the edge.

Speech-to-text: can a small model clean up after the recogniser?

rules + our modelrules alone

200 commands spoken by Kokoro neural voices. Our 2 MB model lifts every recogniser from 42–57% to 81–88% of commands understood. Word error rates: Whisper-turbo + callsign hint 17%, Moonshine medium 19%, Whisper-turbo 27%, Moonshine small 21%, Whisper base 24%.

An airport it has never seen (Sydney)

aircraft rightinstruction right

The pretrained model already knows airline names from its pre-training, so it generalises. Our from-scratch model only knows what it saw.

💡

Pre-training buys generalisation; more varied data beats more parameters; a GPU turns hours into minutes. That is why the plan is: tiny model for the edge, fine-tuned pretrained model for the cloud and as the teacher.

One pretrained model writes the full command; one GPU serves many players at once.

One L4 serving many players with vLLM

commands per second

With one player the cache is slightly slower (bookkeeping, nothing to share). From 16 players up it pays: at 64, +38% throughput and half the tail latency. vLLM alone made a single command 3× faster than plain Hugging Face generate (26–33 ms vs 97 ms).

💡

There is rarely one answer. The edge solution wins on cost, privacy and latency; the cloud ones win on generalisation. Ship the edge one now, keep the cloud ones as the upgrade path and as teachers for the next edge model.
