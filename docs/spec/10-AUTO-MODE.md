# 10 — Auto mode (the AI on the positions the player is not working)

**Where:** `src/lib/sim/engine.ts` — `aiOn(pos)`, `autoApproachStep` / `aiSequenceArrivals` / `aiDeconflict`, `autoTowerStep`, `autoGroundStep` / `aiBreakDeadlocks`. Settings `autoApproach`, `autoTower`, `autoGround` (the nav-bar **Autopilot** button sets all three) and `autoMode` (the AI takes every position the player is not on). The AI uses the same command API as the player, so every rule, score and readback applies to it.

## Approach (arrivals)

One sequence per runway family (parallel runways within 15° share it). Established / cleared aircraft come first by distance; the rest keep the places they were dealt (a newcomer slots in by its earliest time at the 10 NM gate — the **direct** distance, independent of the leg being flown; two aircraft only swap places when one is a clear 4 minutes earlier). An emergency (low fuel after a long hold) goes to the front. Each aircraft gets a **slot** behind the one ahead: wake spacing (≥ 4 NM, or the wake table + 0.5) plus 2 NM of compression as the leader slows, in time at 150 kt. An arrival still high adds the time its descent needs to its delay.

The **delay** to the slot is absorbed with speed (190 kt inbound, 160 kt with a delay or inside 12 track miles) and a **trombone**: the aircraft joins a downwind 4 NM abeam on its own side, flies outbound to a base point pushed out by the delay (each mile of downwind costs two), turns base to a point 1.7 NM abeam and intercepts on a fixed 30° heading. The base point stays inside the airspace (2.5 NM margin). An arrival lined up on the extended centreline with (almost) no delay flies straight in. Legs are a sticky state machine (`aiMode`: straight · join · direct · downwind · base · intercept); an intercept that reaches the localizer before its clearance follows the localizer in and re-joins the pattern only inside 6 NM. A vector that would turn the aircraft into a neighbour (closest approach < 3.8 NM inside 100 s, less than 1,000 ft apart) is withheld and the leg tried again shortly — 30 s at most, and never within 6 NM of the boundary.

**Levels** form a ladder by place — 3,000 ft for the first, +1,000 ft per place, 9,000 at most — sticky and only ever coming down; a rung an aircraft is already level on is its own whatever its place, and no two aircraft of a sequence hold the same rung (a duplicate is skipped upward as far as the present altitude, one rung higher still when that band is free). Descent to the level inside 30 track miles (7,000 inside 45); on base / straight-in, down to the intercept altitude of the gate (below the glideslope there). No descent or climb is given while another aircraft is, or within 120 s will be, inside 5 NM in the altitude band (`altitudeBlocked`).

**ILS clearance** when converging on the centreline at ≤ 30°, inside 3 NM of it, between 6 and 26 NM, **level at the rung** (the slope comes down to it — an aircraft on the final is never above its rung, so the rung above keeps its 1,000 ft; the glideslope is never climbed to), and every aircraft ahead in the sequence that is committed to the final (established, or on its base / intercept leg) is the required spacing ahead in along-track miles — or clearly behind. Those still on the downwind are not waited for; they take the next slot. From a rung above the lowest only while everybody on a lower rung is still out on the downwind. Requests: check-ins get "radar contact, expect ILS"; higher/lower get "roger, expect … shortly"; anything else "unable, expect vectors" (an aircraft already on its approach gets the same answers).

## Conflict net (`aiDeconflict`)

Every pair the AI is responsible for (approach / departure frequency, not both established; emergencies included) with a predicted closest approach < 3.8 NM inside 100 s (on the heading being turned to) and < 1,000 ft (now or then, or crossing altitudes) gets an avoiding action: a climb stopped 1,000 ft below the other, or a descent stopped 1,000 ft above it (never onto a rung somebody else holds) — on either aircraft; two aircraft level at the same altitude: an arrival steps down 1,000 ft (not below 3,000) or a departure steps up 1,000 ft when that band is free of everybody else (and the other is not on its way there); else the one giving way (a departure; else the later one in the sequence) turns 60–90° away from the other, on the side away from it (toward the inside of the airspace near the boundary). A vertical action only pauses the vectoring 10 s; a turn holds 20 s, renewed while the pair is still within 1,000 ft — 90 s at most, and never within 4 NM of the boundary. Departures climb to the top only when nothing blocks the band, are handed to the centre at 7,500 ft once cleared to the top (or at 80 % of the radius), and once with the centre are climbed on to the cruising level (a departure released level would leave the TMA at an arrival entry level).

## Tower

Take-off / line-up / landing / crossing clearances with the runway rules (crossing runways, parallel and same-runway spacing, continue-approach while the runway is being vacated). `crossingSafe` looks at occupants on **both** ends of a runway and at a take-off clearance given but not yet rolling; the AI additionally waits for a rollout to clear (`strict`) and, for a route that runs *along* the strip (an exit loop, a backtrack), allows the time that takes. A take-off clearance needs usable runway ahead of the holding point (`runwayAheadM` ≥ the roll to Vr + 15 % + 150 m): an aircraft stopped at a crossing hold of its own runway near the far end is not "at the holding point" (the crew query it), and a taxi route's departure entry is the last hold line of the runway on it with enough runway ahead — never a crossing of it on the way round. Runways whose strips touch without their centrelines crossing (a T junction) count as intersecting; a stand that itself lies inside a strip is not an incursion to leave.

## Ground

Start-up / pushback / taxi with "hold short of" the first runway on the route; taxi in to the stand; check-ins. **Deadlocks** (an aircraft held 40 s behind one that is standing still — holding short, or held in turn, typically two aircraft nose to nose on one taxiway with one of them on the runway; or two aircraft holding short of the same runway from opposite sides of one taxiway, each on the other's far side) are broken by re-routing the held aircraft (at a crossing: the one that stopped last) around the other and clear of everybody else standing on the taxiways (`planTaxi` with avoid nodes), once per two minutes. Arrivals take a runway exit on the side their stand is on, so the taxi in does not cross the landing runway again. A path that enters a runway strip before its hold line gets a hold at the strip edge (a later hold line does not cover it).

## Runway configuration

The home page switches on the runway family in use for the field's prevailing wind (`preferredEnds`); the rest can be added by hand. All ends on (the old default) put opposite directions in use at once and made the AI's job impossible.

## Testing

`tests/engine/autopilot.test.ts` runs four airports for 40 sim-minutes under the store's traffic cadence and asserts arrivals landed, no diversions and at most one separation loss / go-around / ground conflict. For a wider sweep use the same harness with `ICAO`/`SEED`/`MIN` (see the memory note): events of interest are `separation_loss`, `ground_conflict`, `go_around`, `diversion`.
